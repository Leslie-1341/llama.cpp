You are legislative counsel in the state of East Dakota. There is a bill currently pending in the East Dakota legislature that will require all East Dakota employers with five or more employees that do not offer a qualified retirement plan to employees to automatically enroll their eligible employees in a new retirement savings vehicle called the Dakota Savers Program (the “Program”). The Program has been designed specifically to encourage and facilitate retirement savings by those without current access to an employer retirement plan. Under the Program, eligible employees will be automatically enrolled and will contribute 5% of their wages to an after-tax Individual Retirement Account (commonly known as a Roth IRA) that is established and managed by the state of East Dakota. An “eligible employee” is defined as any common law employee of an East Dakota employer who is 18 years of age or older. Eligible employees may opt out of participation at any time and may also change their contribution percentage to any amount between 1% and 18% of wages.

For eligible employees who participate in the program, their payroll deductions are contributed to an individual retirement account that is established in their name by the Dakota Savers Program. The Program will establish a default investment for such contributions, but also allow participants to select their own investments from a menu that is offered by the Program.

As the bill is currently drafted, a covered employer has only three obligations under the Program: (1) registering as a covered employer; (2) submitting identifying information for each eligible employee; and (3) facilitating the after-tax payroll deduction for those employees who do not opt out of participation. The bill explicitly states that covered employers shall have no fiduciary responsibilities, reporting and disclosure obligations, nor legal liability of any kind related to Program participation. Covered employers that fail to comply with the three requirements imposed on employers are subject, however, to a fine of $250 per employee per day.

Employers are exempt from participation in the Program if they offer any type of retirement plan that is qualified under section 401(a) of the Internal Revenue Code. The bill does not place any type of substantive requirements on the type or form of qualified retirement plan that exempts an employer from participation in the Program. For example, an employer that offered a section 401(k) plan that allowed employee salary deferrals but no matching or other employer contributions would be exempt from participation in the Program.

You have been asked by the Chair of the East Dakota Senate’s Retirement Policy Committee to draft a memo explaining the likelihood that the Dakota Savers Program, as currently drafted and described above, will be preempted by ERISA. 

Write a brief introduction to this memo along with a table of contents. Academic tone. Concise writing, postgraduate level.
